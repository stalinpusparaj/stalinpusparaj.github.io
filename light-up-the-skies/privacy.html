Privacy Policy

Light Up the Skies · Effective 23 November 2026

The game has no required accounts and is always ad-free. Your settings, drawings, photos, videos, saved birthdays and daily joy entries stay on your device unless you choose to share them. If you let the app read your contacts to import birthdays, that happens on your device and nothing is uploaded. Birthday reminders are scheduled on your device too. If you subscribe to Premium, the app store and our subscription provider, RevenueCat, process that purchase. If you join or host an event with Event Sky, the check-in selfie, photos, videos, voice messages and, where selfie search is on, small face crops shared with that event are stored for it until its delete date (see Event Sky).

This policy explains how the Light Up the Skies app for Android and iOS, and the web version of the game, handle information. "We" means the developer of Light Up the Skies.

Information we do not collect

We do not ask you to create an account or upload your email address, contacts or drawings to us. Your name, photo and fireworks show are uploaded only if you play a Sky Duel (see Sky Duel street boards), and what you share at an event only if you join one with Event Sky (see Event Sky). The app can read birthdays from your contacts if you ask it to (see Contacts), but they are used only on your device and are never sent to us or anyone else.

Information stored on your device

The game saves a few things locally so it remembers you between visits: your settings (for example sound, quality and colour theme), the landmark you chose, your achievements, which crackers you have tried, and whether your Premium subscription is active. It also keeps:

  • Birthdays you save: each person's name, birthday (day and month) and, if you add one, a small photo, whether you type them in or import them.
  • Daily joy: your daily entries (how happy you felt, what you were grateful for and any note), so the app can show your streak and history.
  • Your name on cards: the name you last signed a card with, so the next card is signed for you.

All of this stays on your device. You can remove a birthday in the app at any time, and you can delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its data (on the web, by clearing the site's data in your browser).

Contacts (importing birthdays)

Importing birthdays from your contacts is optional and only happens when you tap Import from contacts in the Birthdays screen. The app first explains what it will read, then your phone asks for permission to access your contacts (on Android the Contacts permission; on iPhone and iPad, Contacts access).

  • What is read: the name, birthday and thumbnail photo of contacts that have a birthday saved. Phone numbers, email addresses and other contact details are not read.
  • Where it goes: the list is shown to you on your device, and only the people you tick are added to your saved birthdays. Nothing from your contacts is uploaded, sent to us or shared with anyone.
  • Your control: you can decline, and you can turn contacts access off at any time in your phone's settings. Birthdays you already added stay in the app until you remove them.
  • Web version: browsers cannot read your phone's contacts. Instead, you can choose a contacts file (.vcf) to import; it is read on your device and is not uploaded.

Notifications (birthday reminders)

If you save birthdays in the Android or iOS app, the app reminds you at 9 am the day before each one with a notification such as "Priya's birthday is tomorrow". Your phone asks for permission to show notifications the first time (on Android 13 and later, and on iPhone and iPad).

  • Reminders are scheduled on your device by your phone's own notification system. There is no push notification service, and no birthday or name is sent to a server.
  • On Android, the app sets your reminders again after your phone restarts or the app is updated, so they are not lost.
  • To stop reminders, remove the birthday in the app, or turn off notifications for Light Up the Skies in your phone's settings.
  • On the web, there are no notifications. You can instead add a birthday to your own calendar: the app creates a calendar file on your device for you to open, and your calendar app handles the reminder.

Camera

AR mode uses your camera only while it is switched on, to show fireworks over your surroundings. The camera image is processed on your device and is not sent to us or anyone else. It is only saved if you choose to take a photo or record a video. Event Sky uses the camera only when you take a selfie or a photo to send to an event (see Event Sky).

Photos and videos

  • Saving: photos and videos you capture are saved to your device's gallery or offered in your device's share sheet. On iOS the app asks for permission to add them to your photo library.
  • Greeting-card photos: if you add a photo to a greeting card, you pick it with your device's photo picker. It is resized on your device and used only in that card.
  • Music: if you add a song to a greeting card, it is played from your device during the show and mixed into videos you save. It is not uploaded and is not included in card links.
  • Sharing: videos, cards and daily joy messages are only shared when you choose to, using your device's share sheet, so you decide who receives them.

Greeting-card links

When you share a greeting card, the recipient's name, your name, the message and any photo are encoded inside the link itself. They are not uploaded to or stored on our servers, but anyone who has the link can open it and see the card, so share it only with people you trust.

Sky Duel street boards

When you finish a Sky Duel, the app posts your result to that landmark's weekly street board so other players can see it, watch your show and challenge it. Each entry holds the name you typed, the photo you added (a small 96×96 picture, only if you chose one), your score, the crackers you used, the timing and position of your taps, and the time it was posted. The board is public: anyone playing the game can see your name and photo there. Leave the name empty and add no photo if you would rather not be shown; you then appear as "Player".

To post, the app signs in to Google Firebase with an anonymous ID created on your phone (no email, phone number or account). The boards are stored in Cloud Firestore in Mumbai, India (Firebase privacy). You can remove your entries any time with Remove my shows in the Sky Duel screen. Players can report a name or photo, and we remove anything offensive. Reports hold only the reported entry and the reporter's anonymous ID.

Duel challenge links work like greeting-card links: your name, photo and show are encoded in the link itself, so anyone who has the link can see them.

Event Sky (check-in and photos at events)

Event Sky lets a host run guest check-in and a live photo wall at a wedding, birthday, party or office event. Guests scan the event's QR code (or type its code) and use the event page in the app or in a web browser. Guests do not sign in or create an account. The host decides to run the event and who helps run it; we provide the service and store the event's information for them. This section explains what is stored, who can see it, how long it is kept and how to delete it.

What is collected from guests

  • Check-in: the name you type, the selfie you take, an optional wish, the function you are at (for events with several) and your language.
  • Photos and videos you send, up to the event's limit, with an optional caption and photo mission. Before anything leaves your phone, photos are made smaller and their location (GPS) and other camera details are removed. Videos are at most 15 seconds long and have no sound.
  • Guestbook: an optional message for the hosts and, on some events, a voice message of up to 60 seconds.
  • RSVP (only if the hosts ask for one): your name, how many people are coming to each function, and, if you add them, a phone number, a food preference and a note.
  • Your consent: that you agreed to the notice shown before you share, that you are 18 or older or a parent or guardian sharing for your child, whether you allow selfie search, and which version of the notice you saw.
  • Reports: if you report a photo, the reason you pick. Nothing in the report says who sent it.
  • On your phone: random codes that prove which check-in and photos are yours (so that only you can delete them), photos waiting to be sent, and your language choice. These stay on your phone.

What is collected from hosts and their team (co-hosts, moderators, photographers and big screens)

  • An anonymous ID created on the device to sign in to Google Firebase (no email, phone number or account), and which role each team member has.
  • The event's settings: title, names, dates and times, functions, photo missions, announcements, an optional note for guests, the contact guests can ask to remove a photo and, on some plans, a logo.
  • Photographers' photos, with the photographer's name as a photo credit (always on free events, by choice on paid ones).
  • For an event set up with a pass bought in the app, the Google Play order number; for an activation code, the code (see Event passes).

Counts, not tracking. How many guests arrived, photos were sent and reactions given, and how often the gallery was opened, photos downloaded, selfie searches made and videos played, are kept as totals per hour for the host's statistics. They carry no names, cookies, advertising IDs or other identifiers.

Who can see it

  • The big screen at the event shows guests' names, selfies, wishes and photos, either straight away or after the hosts check them. The notice before you share says which.
  • The hosts and their team can see everything sent to the event, including photos they chose not to show. Guestbook messages, RSVPs and the consent record are for the hosts and co-hosts only and never appear on the big screen.
  • People with the event code or the gallery link can see the photos and videos the hosts approved, with names and captions. The gallery is encrypted: its key travels inside the link itself (after the # sign, which browsers do not send to any server) or behind a password the host chooses.
  • Copies the host makes: the host can save the photos, a photo book, a best-of video and lists (guests, RSVPs, the consent record and statistics) to their own device and share them. Those copies are theirs to look after, and deleting the event does not delete them.
  • Us: we do not look through events. We open an event's information only to handle a removal request or report sent to us, to remove content that breaks our terms, or to fix a fault.

Where it is stored. In Google Firebase: photos, videos, check-ins, messages, RSVPs and event settings in Cloud Firestore in Mumbai, India (asia-south1); the event's public settings (title, names and times), live screen signals, counts and reactions in the Realtime Database in Singapore (asia-southeast1). Everything travels over encrypted connections (HTTPS). See Firebase privacy. Event pages opened in a web browser come from our website on GitHub Pages (see Web version below).

How long it is kept. Every event shows its delete date before you share, on your check-in status, on the big screen, in the gallery and on its printed table cards.

  • Photos, videos and the gallery are kept until that date: 3 days after a free event, and up to 30 days (Event Pass) or 90 days (Shaadi Pass) after a paid one, as the host chooses.
  • Full-size photos and check-in selfies are kept for 3 days after a free event and 14 days after a paid one. After that, up to 300 highlights the host picks stay full size, and the gallery keeps small previews of the other photos, with the names and captions shown beside them, until the delete date.
  • Everything else (check-ins, guestbook messages, RSVPs, reports and the event's settings) is deleted by the delete date.
  • Each record carries its delete date and is removed after it by the event's own screens and devices and by our clean-up of finished events. This can take a few days after the date.

Deleting what you shared

  • Guests: on the event page, on the phone you checked in with, tap Delete my photos and check-in. It removes your selfie, name, wish, guestbook message and every photo and video you sent, from the big screen straight away and from the gallery within a few minutes. It can't be undone. You can change an RSVP on the event page; to remove one, ask the hosts.
  • From another phone, or for a photo someone else took of you: in the gallery, tap Report this photo and choose "It's me and I want it removed", or ask the hosts. Every event shows a contact for removal requests (paid events must give one). You can also write to us (below).
  • Hosts can hide or delete any photo, remove a guest, delete an RSVP and delete the whole event at any time. Deleting the event removes everything at once.
  • Copies that people saved before a deletion (downloads, the host's exports, screenshots) are outside our control.

Selfie search (only on events where the host switches it on)

  • At check-in, each guest chooses whether selfie search may include their face.
  • To prepare it, the host's device finds faces in the event photos and stores small crops of each person's face with the gallery, encrypted with the gallery's key. Guests who said no are left out: their check-in selfie is compared with the faces on the host's device only to find and remove them.
  • When you search, the face finder runs on your own phone and compares your selfie with those crops there. Your selfie and the measurements of anyone's face are never sent or stored.
  • The face finder (about 7 MB) is downloaded from our website the first time you use it.

Camera and microphone. Event pages use the camera only when you take a selfie or a photo to send, and the microphone only while you record a voice message. In the app, your phone asks for permission first. Voice messages are stored with the event like guestbook messages, for the hosts and co-hosts only, and deleted by the event's delete date.

No advertising, no other use. Event Sky has no ads. We never use event photos, videos, names, voices or messages for advertising, to train AI, or for anything except running that event.

Children. Before sharing, guests confirm they are 18 or older, or a parent or guardian sharing for their child. Please don't share photos of other people's children without asking. Anyone can report a photo that shows a child who shouldn't be shown: the hosts check every report, a photo reported by 3 people is hidden until they do, and you can also write to us.

Event passes. Passes for bigger events are bought in the Android app through Google Play, and RevenueCat records the purchase as described under Premium subscriptions. The order number is kept with the event, where only its host can see it, so that the plan can be checked. Activation codes from us are kept the same way.

Questions and requests. For a photo at an event, the event's own contact (shown before you share) is the quickest. You can also write to gemmyapps@gmail.com with the event code (the 6 letters and numbers under its QR code) and what you would like removed; we reply and act within 30 days.

Premium subscriptions

Premium is an optional auto-renewing subscription sold through Google Play or the Apple App Store. Payments are handled entirely by Google or Apple; we never see your payment details.

To verify, restore and manage subscriptions we use RevenueCat. Each time you open the Android or iOS app, it checks with RevenueCat whether Premium is active, and when you open the subscription screen or buy Premium it fetches the plans and records the purchase. For this the app sends RevenueCat an anonymous app user ID it generates, your purchase and subscription history (empty if you have never subscribed), and basic technical information such as your device model, operating system and app version, country (from the store), and IP address. This is used only to provide and support the subscription. The Android app also gives RevenueCat a few anonymous attributes: the app's language, the phone's memory size (rounded to whole GB), how quickly the first firework appeared, and the utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign values of the Google Play link the app was installed from (see How you found the app). See also the Google Privacy Policy and the Apple Privacy Policy.

You can manage or cancel your subscription at any time in your Google Play or Apple ID subscription settings.

Web version

The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you visit it, GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The web version does not offer purchases.

Usage statistics (Android)

The Android app uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count, anonymously, how the app is used: which screens and features are opened, levels started and finished, light shows played, gems earned and spent, and purchases started and completed. It records this app activity with an app instance ID that Firebase creates on your phone, along with basic device information such as the model, Android version, language and approximate country. It is not linked to your name or account, and never includes names you type, contacts, birthdays, card text, photos or videos. We use it only to improve the game. See Firebase privacy. Deleting the app resets its app instance ID.

How you found the app. Once per install, the Android app from Google Play asks Google Play's Install Referrer service which link it was installed from. Only the link's campaign tags are kept (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content and utm_term, for example "share", "countdown"). They are stored on your phone, recorded in Google Analytics for Firebase as user properties (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign) and one install event, and sent to RevenueCat as attributes (utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign). They say which of our links or campaigns brought the install and are never linked to your name. Installs that did not come from a tagged link record nothing.

Children

The game is suitable for a general audience. Apart from what a player chooses to post to a Sky Duel street board or share at an Event Sky event (see Event Sky), we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Younger players should use a nickname and no photo; a parent can remove a child's entries as described below. If you believe a child has provided personal information through a subscription, contact us and we will help have it removed.

Your choices and rights

Apart from Sky Duel entries and Event Sky events, we hold no personal information ourselves, so most requests can be met by deleting the app's data on your device. For subscription records held by RevenueCat, contact us and we will request their access or deletion on your behalf. Depending on where you live (for example under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act or the EU GDPR), you may have rights to access, correct or delete your data, which you can exercise by contacting us.

Deleting your data

To delete your Light Up the Skies data:

  • Data on your device (settings, birthdays, daily joy entries, achievements, drawings): remove birthdays in the app, or delete everything at once by uninstalling Light Up the Skies or clearing its data in your phone's settings. This is immediate, and nothing is kept.
  • Sky Duel street board entries: tap Remove my shows in the Sky Duel screen, which deletes them at once, or email us and we will delete them within 30 days.
  • Event Sky check-ins, photos and messages: tap Delete my photos and check-in on the event page, report the photo in the gallery, or ask the event's hosts; see Deleting what you shared. Or email us with the event code and we will delete them within 30 days.
  • Subscription records held by RevenueCat (the anonymous app user ID, your purchase and subscription history, and the technical information listed under Premium subscriptions): email gemmyapps@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my Light Up the Skies data". If you can, include the date and store of your purchase so we can find your record. We will ask RevenueCat to delete it and confirm by email within 30 days.

Google Play and the Apple App Store keep their own records of your purchases under their privacy policies; we cannot delete those. Deleting your data does not cancel an active subscription, so cancel it first in your Google Play or Apple ID subscription settings.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date.
