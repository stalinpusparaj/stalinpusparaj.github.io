Privacy Policy

Light Up the Skies · Effective 27 October 2026

The game has no required accounts. The free Android app uses Google AdMob banner ads and optional rewarded videos; Premium users do not receive ads. Your settings, drawings, photos, videos, saved birthdays and daily joy entries stay on your device unless you choose to share them. If you let the app read your contacts to import birthdays, that happens on your device and nothing is uploaded. Birthday reminders are scheduled on your device too. If you subscribe to Premium, the app store and our subscription provider, RevenueCat, process that purchase.

This policy explains how the Light Up the Skies app for Android and iOS, and the web version of the game, handle information. "We" means the developer of Light Up the Skies.

Information we do not collect

We do not ask you to create an account or upload your email address, contacts or drawings to us. Your name, photo and fireworks show are uploaded only if you play a Sky Duel (see Sky Duel street boards). The free Android app includes Google AdMob, which processes advertising and technical information as described below. The app can read birthdays from your contacts if you ask it to (see Contacts), but they are used only on your device and are never sent to us or anyone else.

Information stored on your device

The game saves a few things locally so it remembers you between visits: your settings (for example sound, quality and colour theme), the landmark you chose, your achievements, which crackers you have tried, and whether your Premium subscription is active. It also keeps:

  • Birthdays you save: each person's name, birthday (day and month) and, if you add one, a small photo, whether you type them in or import them.
  • Daily joy: your daily entries (how happy you felt, what you were grateful for and any note), so the app can show your streak and history.
  • Your name on cards: the name you last signed a card with, so the next card is signed for you.

All of this stays on your device. You can remove a birthday in the app at any time, and you can delete everything by uninstalling the app or clearing its data (on the web, by clearing the site's data in your browser).

Contacts (importing birthdays)

Importing birthdays from your contacts is optional and only happens when you tap Import from contacts in the Birthdays screen. The app first explains what it will read, then your phone asks for permission to access your contacts (on Android the Contacts permission; on iPhone and iPad, Contacts access).

  • What is read: the name, birthday and thumbnail photo of contacts that have a birthday saved. Phone numbers, email addresses and other contact details are not read.
  • Where it goes: the list is shown to you on your device, and only the people you tick are added to your saved birthdays. Nothing from your contacts is uploaded, sent to us or shared with anyone.
  • Your control: you can decline, and you can turn contacts access off at any time in your phone's settings. Birthdays you already added stay in the app until you remove them.
  • Web version: browsers cannot read your phone's contacts. Instead, you can choose a contacts file (.vcf) to import; it is read on your device and is not uploaded.

Notifications (birthday reminders)

If you save birthdays in the Android or iOS app, the app reminds you at 9 am the day before each one with a notification such as "Priya's birthday is tomorrow". Your phone asks for permission to show notifications the first time (on Android 13 and later, and on iPhone and iPad).

  • Reminders are scheduled on your device by your phone's own notification system. There is no push notification service, and no birthday or name is sent to a server.
  • On Android, the app sets your reminders again after your phone restarts or the app is updated, so they are not lost.
  • To stop reminders, remove the birthday in the app, or turn off notifications for Light Up the Skies in your phone's settings.
  • On the web, there are no notifications. You can instead add a birthday to your own calendar: the app creates a calendar file on your device for you to open, and your calendar app handles the reminder.

Camera

AR mode uses your camera only while it is switched on, to show fireworks over your surroundings. The camera image is processed on your device and is not sent to us or anyone else. It is only saved if you choose to take a photo or record a video.

Photos and videos

  • Saving: photos and videos you capture are saved to your device's gallery or offered in your device's share sheet. On iOS the app asks for permission to add them to your photo library.
  • Greeting-card photos: if you add a photo to a greeting card, you pick it with your device's photo picker. It is resized on your device and used only in that card.
  • Music: if you add a song to a greeting card, it is played from your device during the show and mixed into videos you save. It is not uploaded and is not included in card links.
  • Sharing: videos, cards and daily joy messages are only shared when you choose to, using your device's share sheet, so you decide who receives them.

Greeting-card links

When you share a greeting card, the recipient's name, your name, the message and any photo are encoded inside the link itself. They are not uploaded to or stored on our servers, but anyone who has the link can open it and see the card, so share it only with people you trust.

Sky Duel street boards

When you finish a Sky Duel, the app posts your result to that landmark's weekly street board so other players can see it, watch your show and challenge it. Each entry holds the name you typed, the photo you added (a small 96×96 picture, only if you chose one), your score, the crackers you used, the timing and position of your taps, and the time it was posted. The board is public: anyone playing the game can see your name and photo there. Leave the name empty and add no photo if you would rather not be shown; you then appear as "Player".

To post, the app signs in to Google Firebase with an anonymous ID created on your phone (no email, phone number or account). The boards are stored in Cloud Firestore in Mumbai, India (Firebase privacy). You can remove your entries any time with Remove my shows in the Sky Duel screen. Players can report a name or photo, and we remove anything offensive. Reports hold only the reported entry and the reporter's anonymous ID.

Duel challenge links work like greeting-card links: your name, photo and show are encoded in the link itself, so anyone who has the link can see them.

Premium subscriptions

Premium is an optional auto-renewing subscription sold through Google Play or the Apple App Store. Payments are handled entirely by Google or Apple; we never see your payment details.

To verify, restore and manage subscriptions we use RevenueCat. Each time you open the Android or iOS app, it checks with RevenueCat whether Premium is active, and when you open the subscription screen or buy Premium it fetches the plans and records the purchase. For this the app sends RevenueCat an anonymous app user ID it generates, your purchase and subscription history (empty if you have never subscribed), and basic technical information such as your device model, operating system and app version, country (from the store), and IP address. This is used only to provide and support the subscription. See also the Google Privacy Policy and the Apple Privacy Policy.

You can manage or cancel your subscription at any time in your Google Play or Apple ID subscription settings.

Web version

The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages. When you visit it, GitHub may log technical data such as your IP address under the GitHub Privacy Statement. The web version does not offer purchases.

Advertising and reward videos (Android)

The free Android app uses Google AdMob to display banner ads and optional reward videos. Google Mobile Ads may collect and share IP addresses (which can indicate approximate location), device and advertising identifiers, app interactions, crash logs and diagnostic information for advertising, fraud prevention and service operation. Our birthday, contact, drawing and greeting-card content is not supplied to the advertising SDK.

Where required, Google's User Messaging Platform presents privacy choices before ad requests. You can reopen the available choices using Ad privacy choices in Settings or the rewards panel. A confirmed rewarded video grants 150 in-game points. Simply closing an ad does not grant a reward. Earned receipt IDs and points are stored on the device to recover earned rewards and avoid granting the same reward twice.

Premium users do not receive banner or rewarded ads. These AdMob ads are not loaded in the browser or iOS version. See Google's Privacy Policy and Google Mobile Ads data disclosures.

Children

The game is suitable for a general audience. Apart from what a player chooses to post to a Sky Duel street board, we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. Younger players should use a nickname and no photo; a parent can remove a child's entries as described below. If you believe a child has provided personal information through a subscription, contact us and we will help have it removed.

Your choices and rights

Because we hold no personal information ourselves, most requests can be met by deleting the app's data on your device. For subscription records held by RevenueCat, contact us and we will request their access or deletion on your behalf. Depending on where you live (for example under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act or the EU GDPR), you may have rights to access, correct or delete your data, which you can exercise by contacting us.

Deleting your data

To delete your Light Up the Skies data:

  • Data on your device (settings, birthdays, daily joy entries, achievements, drawings): remove birthdays in the app, or delete everything at once by uninstalling Light Up the Skies or clearing its data in your phone's settings. This is immediate, and nothing is kept.
  • Sky Duel street board entries: tap Remove my shows in the Sky Duel screen, which deletes them at once, or email us and we will delete them within 30 days.
  • Subscription records held by RevenueCat (the anonymous app user ID, your purchase and subscription history, and the technical information listed under Premium subscriptions): email gemmyapps@gmail.com with the subject "Delete my Light Up the Skies data". If you can, include the date and store of your purchase so we can find your record. We will ask RevenueCat to delete it and confirm by email within 30 days.

Google Play and the Apple App Store keep their own records of your purchases under their privacy policies; we cannot delete those. Deleting your data does not cancel an active subscription, so cancel it first in your Google Play or Apple ID subscription settings.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the game handles information, we will update this page and its effective date.
